About

I’ve always been curious about how things work, whether they’re built by us or by nature.

I like understanding things from the ground up. Sometimes that means figuring out how a piece of technology works, and other times it's trying to understand something much more complicated, like how living systems respond to their environment. What excites me most is when ideas from seemingly unrelated fields come together to solve a problem.

Today, I'm a Ph.D. researcher at Cornell University, working at the intersection of artificial intelligence, computer vision, and scientific research. Much of my work has involved plants and environmental systems, but my interests go well beyond any one application. I'm fascinated by how the same computational ideas can help us understand entirely different problems, especially in areas where we still have more questions than answers.

I enjoy the entire process of research, from learning something completely unfamiliar to building a solution from scratch, testing it, realizing what doesn't work, and figuring out why. I don't necessarily want to spend my career solving just one kind of problem. I'd rather keep learning, exploring new fields, and working on things that I find genuinely interesting.

Beyond the Lab

Outside research, I enjoy music, cooking, photography, and movies. I also have a somewhat contradictory personality: I can spend an unreasonable amount of time perfecting one tiny detail while being surprisingly carefree about something else.

Depending on the day, I’m either an overly excited kid discovering something new or a philosophical old man contemplating life. Sometimes both in the same afternoon.

The Winding Road

A few key turns in a journey of connecting engineering, biology, and AI.

  1. Early Years

    Circuits, Curiosity & Biology

    I loved tinkering with breadboards, LEDs, and batteries, but was equally fascinated by biology and living systems. Both interests were always part of my curiosity.

  2. Undergrad

    Exploring Different Worlds

    While studying electrical engineering, I also explored finance, investment banking, and digital marketing, driven by an interest in entrepreneurship.

  3. Grad School

    Finding the Connection

    Discovering machine learning gave me a way to combine engineering, computation, biology, and the environment into something I genuinely wanted to pursue.

  4. Ph.D. & Beyond

    Still Connecting the Dots

    My journey brought me to Cornell, where I now explore how AI can help answer scientific questions, particularly in complex biological and environmental systems.

What Keeps Me Curious

  • Learning Something New

    The thrill of entering a field I know little about and slowly figuring it out.

  • Building From Scratch

    Give me an interesting problem and a blank page, and I’ll likely lose track of time.

  • Connecting the Dots

    Bringing ideas from different disciplines together, especially when new possibilities emerge.

  • Making Things Useful

    I love exploring ideas, but it’s even more exciting when they turn into something that works in the real world.

Looking Ahead

I’d love to lead R&D efforts to develop technologies that make a meaningful difference, and at some point, start a company of my own.

Until then, I plan to keep learning, experimenting, building, and following interesting questions wherever they lead.
